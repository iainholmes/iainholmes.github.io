## Provisional issue concept
**Working hero:** *Where Risk Becomes Visible*  
**Editorial tension:** Economic decisions change when the distribution, location, or time horizon of risk becomes measurable. A new satellite/ML measurement approach makes industrial emissions-related activity observable; farm-robot scenarios reframe costs; a fire-risk model separates hazard from built exposure; resource economics compares aquifer depletion against an intertemporal optimum; and pollution-monitoring research links information salience with financial-market valuation. ## Five reading candidates (not yet substantively cleared)

### 01 · Satellite signals reveal hidden steel production
- **ID:** \`2026-11-06-steel-measurement\`
- **Primary:** Yang, Ai & Arkolakis, *A geospatial approach to measuring economic activity*, *Nature Communications*, early peer-reviewed article, 3 November 2026. https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-78786-1 (DOI: 10.1038/s41467-026-78786-1).
- **Publicly reported evidence:** ML combines air pollution, nighttime light, temperature and plant-output data to detect locations and predict steel output. Reported identification of ~88% of otherwise missing plants within 1 km and ~94% within 5 km; reported R² > 0.8 for output predictions in the defined validation settings. **These are prediction/detection metrics, not experimental treatment effects or pollution-response elasticities.**
- **Critical version check:** NBER Working Paper 33619 (March 2025) abstract reported ~40%/~70% under earlier methodology. Do **not** copy the 2025 figures into the 2026 article, and do not silently treat the two versions as identical. Review the new paper's validation design, train/test splits, external validity claims in Iran/South Korea, and full exact denominators.
- **Potential features:** Economic Figures (detector performance by radius with point values and no invented uncertainty); Assumption Test (transportability and the distinction between industrial-output proxies and measured pollution damages).
- **Economic interpretation:** Lower monitoring costs can change environmental enforcement and measurement of informal industrial activity. Accuracy in predicting physical output does not alone establish net benefits of expanded monitoring.
- **Source review state:** *Partial primary public text/abstract inspected; complete method, results, appendices and version hashes not yet authenticated.* No verified component attestation.

### 02 · Farm autonomy shifts the variable-input margin
- **ID:** \`2026-11-06-farm-robots\`
- **Primary:** Schlehuber et al., *Techno-economic and environmental assessment of next-generation battery-powered farming robots*, *Nature Communications*, early peer-reviewed article, 2 November 2026. https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-78281-7 (DOI: 10.1038/s41467-026-78281-7).
- **Publicly reported evidence:** Total-cost-of-ownership plus life-cycle assessment of a small autonomous battery-powered farm robot for strawberries, onions, lettuce and peanuts under organic and nonorganic modeled management. Costs across benchmarked scenarios range from 76% below to 17% above literature reference ranges; variable inputs account for 72–90% of costs and 72–95% of global-warming impacts. **The outcomes are scenario comparisons, not measured average treatment effects of robot adoption.**
- **Critical checks:** Compare scenario assumptions, technology availability, omitted fixed costs, farm scale, equipment lifetime, electricity mix, labor opportunity cost, crop heterogeneity, and whether modeled cost savings are causally attributed to automation or induced changes in agronomic inputs.
- **Potential features:** The Decision Margin (input and labor savings needed to offset platform costs); The Incidence Ledger (farmer capital expense, variable inputs, labor impacts, environmental impacts, qualified as modeled).
- **Source review state:** *Partial primary public text/abstract inspected; full scenario tables, supplement and model assumptions not yet checked.* No verified component attestation.

### 03 · Rising fire losses reflect exposure as well as hazard
- **ID:** \`2026-11-06-structure-fire-risk\`
- **Primary:** Iglesias et al., *Fire risk to U.S. structures nearly triples by mid-century*, *Nature Communications* 17, 10960, 3 November 2026. https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-78904-z (DOI: 10.1038/s41467-026-78904-z). Open-access full main text available.
- **Source distinctions confirmed:** The paper reconstructs structural risk from 1945–2023, projects through 2060, and decomposes hazard, building exposure and vulnerability. Three future climate-model outcomes imply 2051–2060 risk factors of 3.2, 2.27, and 1.85 relative to 2011–2020; the paper reports a 2024–2060 modeled cumulative range of 133,200–449,100 buildings damaged or destroyed. Risk is **expected structure count, not property-value damages**. The business-as-usual scenario holds key vulnerability/structural factors constant and assumes no adaptation. Distinguish the 30% hardship overlap *among specified high-risk western tracts* from a 30% nationwide claim.
- **Economic mechanisms:** Land-use incentives and building decisions change long-run exposure; pricing/insurance, mitigation, development regulation and public infrastructure affect adaptation options. The risk model does not estimate the causal effect of any such policy, nor direct welfare.
- **Potential features:** The Long View (1945–2060 exposure and evolving expected risk, clearly labeled historical vs modeled); The Incidence Ledger or Mechanism Map (hazard × exposure × vulnerability and uneven social capacity).
- **Source review state:** *Substantial open main text inspected, including methods and scenarios; supplement, independent numerical replication and all component mappings still pending.* No verified component attestation.

### 04 · Groundwater's efficient path is not a frozen water table
- **ID:** \`2026-11-06-groundwater-depletion\`
- **Primary:** Greaney, Shapiro & Wagner, *Are We Consuming Too Much Groundwater?*, Federal Reserve Bank of Dallas Research Working Paper 2626 (August 2026); October 2026 NBER WP 35904. Complete freely accessible Dallas Fed PDF: https://www.dallasfed.org/~/media/documents/research/papers/2026/wp2626.pdf . DOI: https://doi.org/10.24149/wp2626
- **Primary passages directly inspected:** PDF printed pp. 1–2 (abstract and model setup); printed p. 11 (§3.3): identification of aquifer-specific decision discount factors using dynamic value-function iteration and observed extraction; opening of §4 on stock, recharge and marginal extraction cost data. Main claim: roughly three-fifths to four-fifths of analyzed aquifers are over-extracted compared with an estimated optimum, at model-based global present-value welfare costs in the trillions. **This is structural model calibration/estimation, not a randomized conservation-policy evaluation.**
- **Critical qualifications:** Optimization uses assumed production functions, marginal pumping costs, recharge measurement and a normative benchmark discount factor. The source notes excluded aquifers where no feasible implied private discount factor reproduces observed extraction, and evaluates sensitivity separately. Constant future aquifer fill is not generally efficient when stocks are already below optimal levels. Do not invent exact welfare figures from rounded abstract language.
- **Potential features:** The Estimation Desk (source-authored Bellman/stock-flow equations only after exact tokens and source notation are verified); The Long View or Assumption Test (compare estimated observed extraction, no-future-discount and welfare-optimal extraction with explicit benchmarks).
- **Source review state:** *Targeted sections of full PDF inspected including two rendered page images; remaining full paper and supplementary appendix not yet fully reviewed.* No verified component attestation.

### 05 · Pollution disclosure can change information production and prices
- **ID:** \`2026-11-06-pollution-asset-pricing\`
- **Primary:** Kala, Sani, Shroff & Zhang, *Pollution Monitoring and the Pricing of Environmental Risk in Firms*, NBER Working Paper 35969 (October 2026); DOI 10.3386/w35969. https://www.nber.org/papers/w35969 ; author-posted SSRN draft: https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7595698 .
- **Useful author-authored exposition:** Nemit Shroff, ECGI, *How does pollution monitoring shape analysts’ pricing of environmental risks?*, 8 September 2026: https://www.ecgi.global/publications/blog/how-does-pollution-monitoring-shape-analysts-pricing-of-environmental-risks .
- **Source claims needing direct paper confirmation:** Staggered ambient-pollution monitoring introduction in China as an analyst-attention exposure; treated analysts' pollution-related attention, site visits, forecast revisions and green/brown price responses. Firm classification, parallel-trends/exclusion assumptions, standard errors and permanence inference require the actual full paper. Two publicly circulating writeups give different market-capitalization totals; **do not publish a numerical magnitude without reconciling document versions and definitions**.
- **Potential features:** The Mechanism Map (monitoring salience → analyst information → forecast revisions → price reaction, distinguish evidence links from inference); The Assumption Test (timing of monitoring and same-firm/same-date comparison, alternative local information shocks).
- **Source review state:** *NBER abstract and author-authored explanatory article inspected; SSRN full 99-page text not yet substantively reviewed.* This candidate must remain withheld from a verified new reading until the primary source review is complete.
