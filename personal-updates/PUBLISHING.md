## Shared scholarly clarity review — all three publications

**Intellectual sophistication without unnecessary linguistic complexity.** Read this standard before drafting any future Field Brief, Workbook or Loblolly & Logit edition. Apply it during the existing editorial and source review, before freezing the new edition and its artwork. It governs clarity, not stylistic uniformity; it does not authorize revisions to published editions, including recovery or verification of an already-published issue.

- **Sentence construction:** Make subjects, verbs and logical relationships readily identifiable. Unpack dense noun clusters, ambiguous modifiers and unnecessarily delayed main clauses. Split competing independent ideas where useful; retain long or sophisticated sentences when they improve precision, rhythm or argument.
- **Attribution and evidence:** Name institutions, researchers and publications in natural grammatical constructions. Distinguish what a source reports, argues, estimates, demonstrates or hypothesizes according to the evidence, rather than mechanically substituting verbs. Keep a source's own claim distinct from independently established evidence and the publication's interpretation. Preserve proposed/adopted status, conditional forecasts, observational associations, identification assumptions and the limits of causal claims.
- **Academic precision:** Retain useful disciplinary vocabulary, mathematics, statistical distinctions and appropriate academic register. Give unfamiliar terms enough context for the intended reader; do not replace them with vague everyday approximations. Preserve uncertainty, qualifications, competing interpretations and methodological limitations.
- **First careful reading:** Before approving an article, summary or introductory paragraph, check that an attentive reader can identify (1) its principal subject or finding, (2) the source or basis of the claim, (3) how the evidence supports or limits the interpretation, and (4) why it matters. Assess the passage in context, not every sentence in isolation. A Workbook argument may rest on stated assumptions or a derivation rather than an empirical source. Revise avoidable structural difficulty without lowering conceptual complexity or adding repetitive explanatory boilerplate.

Keep each publication's voice and existing content roles:

| Publication | Apply the review to new material | Preserve |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Field Brief | Introductory copy, story summaries, significance, implications and synthesis | Clear, authoritative reporting and efficient explanations; distinguish reporting from editorial interpretation. |
| The Workbook | Question stems, exhibits, answer choices, Assistance and Explanation | Graduate-level methodological rigor, notation, assumptions, units and technical vocabulary. Clarity edits must retain the intended answer and reasoning; Assistance must still stop short of disclosing the answer. |
| Loblolly & Logit | Findings, economic interpretations, evidence limits, synthesis and new notebook or companion prose | Analytical depth, personality, literary rhythm and distinctive phrasing; no requirement to adopt Field Brief's brevity. |

For a passage revised for clarity, compare the draft and revision against the source or worked reasoning. Verify unchanged attribution, quantities, units, time frame, scope, uncertainty, assumptions and strength of inference. A clearer sentence must not turn an association into a causal effect, a scenario into a forecast, a proposal into enacted policy, or non-rejection into evidence of no effect. Clear technical or literary prose may pass unchanged.

Record a concise completion note in the existing publication QA/release report, for example: “Scholarly clarity reviewed: attribution, evidence/interpretation, qualifications and publication voice checked.” Do not add a required archive field, retrofit historical review records or invent a separate approval workflow. Resolve concrete ambiguity, misattribution or unsupported inference through the existing editorial/source review; do not hold a release for optional stylistic preferences or pursue endless polishing. Existing factual, learning-content and publication-integrity safeguards remain authoritative.

This is a required **publisher instruction and editorial review criterion**, not an automated judgment of prose. The repository has structural/content validators but no reliable validator of scholarly comprehension; their passing output does not certify this review. There are no readability-score thresholds, sentence-length limits, vocabulary bans, automated rewrites or new subjective style gates. Schedules, source requirements and release checks remain unchanged. The existing page maintenance instructions for all three titles already refer publishers to this contract; external scheduled-task settings are not changed by it.

See [the review examples](SCHOLARLY-CLARITY-EXAMPLES.md) for revision exercises and passages that should remain intact.
