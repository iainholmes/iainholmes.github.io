# Scholarly clarity — review examples

Companion to the [shared publishing standard](PUBLISHING.md#shared-scholarly-clarity-review--all-three-publications). These examples exercise editorial judgment, not an automated acceptance test or new publication template.

The review used canonical Field Brief editions from October 19–21, 2026, Workbook Set 012 from October 16, and Loblolly & Logit Issues 003–004. No publication text was edited. “Draft exercise” passages below are deliberately constructed, not quotations from the journal or proposed replacements for published text. The October 13 PFAS passage is outside these exercises and remains untouched.

## 1. Field Brief — institutional attribution and dense nouns

Basis: [Field Brief source](../daily-watchlist-5/index.html), October 19, reading 03, FERC's proposed LNG authorization procedures.

**Draft exercise:** “FERC's eligible LNG-facility capacity-increase prior-notice authorization proposal would replace some individual orders with notice procedures.”

**Revision:** “FERC proposes allowing some eligible capacity increases at LNG facilities to proceed through prior-notice procedures rather than individual authorization orders. The proposal has not been adopted.”

**Review:** Revise. The institution performs an identifiable action, and the procedural change becomes legible without a noun stack. Retain “some,” “eligible,” the proposal's status and the distinction between notice procedures and individual orders. Do not imply approval of every expansion or an observed economic benefit. Significance belongs in the surrounding reporting and implications, not an invented causal conclusion.

## 2. Field Brief — uncertainty that must survive editing

Basis: the same source, October 21, reading 02, UNEP's emissions report.

**Published passage:** “UNEP warns that the return pathway also depends on large-scale carbon removal whose cost, permanence and social consequences remain uncertain.”

**Result:** Retain unchanged.

**Review:** The attribution, dependency and uncertainty are clear on a careful reading. The surrounding story supplies the temperature pathway and emissions figures. “Carbon removal will return warming to 1.5°C” would erase uncertainty and turn a conditional pathway into a promised outcome. Shortening the sentence is not itself an improvement.

## 3. The Workbook — technical meaning with clearer syntax

Basis: [Workbook source](../daily-econ-challenge/index.html), Set 012, Q1, dynamic panels and Nickell bias.

**Draft exercise:** “With fixed T, within-transformation lagged-dependent-variable regressor–demeaned-error correlation produces order-1/T bias that increasing N does not eliminate.”

**Revision:** “In a dynamic panel with fixed T, demeaning correlates the lagged dependent variable with the transformed error. The resulting Nickell bias is of order 1/T and does not vanish as N grows.”

**Review:** Revise. Retain the transformation, lagged regressor, fixed-T asymptotic setting and bias order. The set's context identifies N as the number of units and T as observations per unit. “More data remove the bias” would incorrectly conflate a larger cross-section with a longer panel. Technical terminology is necessary here.

## 4. The Workbook — necessary identification qualifications

Basis: Set 012, Q1, post-answer Explanation.

**Published passage** (mathematical HTML transcribed into notation): “Differencing eliminates αᵢ; if the level errors are not serially correlated, levels dated t−2 and earlier can be valid instruments for Δyᵢ,ₜ₋₁.”

**Result:** Retain unchanged.

**Review:** The conditional clause and “can be” matter: instrument validity depends on the model's moment conditions. Retain the lag restrictions and distinguish eliminating a fixed effect from establishing orthogonality. Reject “Differencing makes lagged levels valid instruments,” which removes the condition and claims too much. Serial-correlation diagnostics assess implications of the moment conditions; passing them is not proof of causal identification.

## 5. The Workbook — well-written quantitative reasoning

Basis: Set 012, Q2, post-answer Explanation; square-root utility, wealth 100, loss 64 with probability 0.25, and full-insurance premium 16.

**Published reasoning** (mathematical HTML transcribed into notation): “Without insurance, expected wealth is also 84, but expected utility is 0.75√100 + 0.25√36 = 9, while √84 ≈ 9.165. Strict concavity therefore makes full insurance strictly preferred.”

**Result:** Retain unchanged in Explanation.

**Review:** The calculation explains the preference while distinguishing expected utility from expected wealth. Preserve the quantities and strict-concavity assumption; “Insurance pays more” would be inaccurate. This is post-answer teaching, not material to move into Assistance. Clarity must preserve the existing learning sequence.

## 6. Loblolly & Logit — attribution without overstating the research

Basis: [L&L source](../weekly-economics-environment/index.html), Issue 004, `2026-10-16-targeted-power`, Jones and Pineda-Torres's power analysis.

**Draft exercise:** “The Jones–Pineda-Torres small-subgroup population-data policy-effect detection assessment calculates detectable effects rather than a new policy effect.”

**Revision:** “Jones and Pineda-Torres calculate how large an effect would need to be for population data to detect it when a policy directly affects only a small subgroup. They do not estimate a new causal policy effect.”

**Review:** Revise. The researchers, question and scope of the calculation are identifiable. Preserve the distinction between statistical power under stated assumptions and an estimated policy effect. A null result does not establish no effect, and a power calculation does not establish an effect. The surrounding article retains the working-paper status, data basis and planning assumptions.

## 7. Loblolly & Logit — literary phrasing with analytical purpose

Basis: Issue 003, `2026-10-09-connections`, the incidence synthesis.

**Published sentence:** “A shock does not arrive with its final payer attached.”

**Result:** Retain unchanged.

**Review:** The sentence introduces the distinction between an initial shock and its ultimate economic incidence; the synthesis develops the transmission mechanisms. It does not need an institution, citation or literal definition embedded in the sentence itself. Replacing it with a glossary definition would flatten the voice without improving comprehension. Assess the paragraph and argument in context.

## 8. Loblolly & Logit — simplification that would distort inference

Basis: Issue 004, `2026-10-16-targeted-power`, evidence limitations.

**Published sentence:** “Non-rejection is not proof of no effect, but low power does not prove an effect exists either.”

**Result:** Retain unchanged.

**Rejected simplification:** “The policy probably has an effect, but the dataset is too small to find it.”

**Review:** The original distinguishes absence of evidence from evidence of absence and avoids inferring an effect from low power. The rejected version introduces an unsupported probability judgment and attributes the outcome to sample size alone. Retain the surrounding qualifications about treatment prevalence, dependence, timing, heterogeneity and specification.

## Review outcome and limits

Three constructed drafts benefited from clearer sentence structure; five published passages or mathematical transcriptions were retained. The review preserved attribution, conditional status, technical vocabulary, calculations, uncertainty and L&L's literary voice. It did not rewrite any published edition, change an answer or insert extra claims.

These are worked editorial examples, not evidence that software can assess comprehension. Existing validators can check publication structure, authored learning content, sources, archives and artwork; the publisher must perform the scholarly clarity review. No scoring rule, sentence-length limit, prohibited-word list, new metadata field or publication-blocking style classifier is introduced.
