APeriodicals

Periodicals is a small set of recurring publications made for one reader. Each has its own subject, schedule and design; they share a front page, a navigation shelf and a few conventions described here.

Every issue is kept. Nothing is replaced by the newest edition: each title keeps a cumulative archive in its own page, so past issues remain readable at stable addresses.

BThe three titles

Field Brief

Daily · 08:00 ET · /daily-watchlist-5/

Five consequential developments each morning across AI and technology, climate and energy, U.S. policy, and North Carolina and the Triangle, chosen by consequence rather than quota. Each story gives what happened, why it matters and its implications, with sources.

  • Field Brief is the publication; Daily Briefing is the masthead of each day’s edition.
  • Index (top right) slides in the full archive by month; open an issue to see its five headlines and jump straight to any story.
  • Links such as #2026-09-27 open an issue; #2026-09-27/03 opens its third story.

Loblolly & Logit

Fridays · 15:00 ET · /weekly-economics-environment/

Weekly journal covering environmental economics and applied research: about five developments in environmental, resource, energy and climate economics, policy, working papers, methods and data, treated at graduate level: identification, limitations, incidence and welfare.

  • Full edition reads every article; At a glance shows only the title page, contents and Field Notes.
  • Issues opens the searchable archive of past Fridays.
  • Each article carries a research note in the margin and an explicit statement of what the evidence does and does not show; the methods notebook at the end collects the concepts used.
  • Field Notes, the Field Register and the Heron are described in sections F and G.

The Workbook

Weekdays · 10:00 ET · /daily-econ-challenge/

Economics and econometrics practice: about eight questions per set across micro, macro, environmental economics, causal inference, econometrics and statistics, as single-select, multi-select or true/false.

  • Answer with the mouse or keyboard: A–D choose, T/F for true/false, ← → move, Enter advances or submits.
  • After submitting you get a score, a tally by field, concepts to review and a worked solution for every question, including why each wrong option fails. Copy result gives a one-line record.
  • The Ledger lists every set with your record for each. Progress is saved in this browser only.

CMoving around

The shelf. Every title has a small Periodicals mark at the top left. It opens a row of book spines, one per title, with the one you are reading raised. Choose a spine to move; Esc or a click elsewhere closes it. The same row carries a link to this handbook.

The front page remembers. The title you visited most recently stands slightly raised on the Periodicals shelf, so returning is one glance. The order of the titles never changes.

Each title’s own archive sits in its top bar: Index in Field Brief, Issues in Loblolly & Logit, Ledger in The Workbook. Each opens with the latest issue; older issues are one choice away and every issue has its own link.

DIssue folios

Issues are numbered in publication order: No.001, No.002, and so on. The number counts published issues, not calendar days, so a skipped day or week never leaves a gap, and published numbers are never changed.

On the Periodicals front page folios always have three digits. Inside a title the same superscript “No.” convention is set in that title’s own type; Loblolly & Logit also keeps a journal-style volume line.

EDay / Night

The three titles share one reading preference. Choose Day or Night in any of them and the others open the same way, including tabs already open. Each interprets Night in its own materials rather than inverting colors:

FField Notes & Field Register

Loblolly & Logit introduces the real people and institutions behind environmental economics: economists and researchers, labs, agencies, nonprofits and others whose work the week’s issue touches. They appear in two forms.

Field Notes belong to the current issue: two or three profiles set side by side in the green band below the contents, each with a large plate, what the person or institution does, and specifically where economics enters their work, with a source and a link to the related article.

The Field Register is the permanent directory. Every entry from every issue accumulates there, numbered in order, searchable and grouped by issue. Open it from the Field Notes band, from the footer, or with the link #field-register.

Entries are factual and sourced. Pictures are used only when they are openly licensed or an organization’s own identifying mark, with credit; otherwise an engraved plate drawn from the name stands in.

GThe Heron

The Heron is a reader companion that lives in the margins of Loblolly & Logit. On a few passages each week it flies in, lands beside the text, and waits. If you ask, it tells you something useful about what you are reading.

Why a heron, and why a body at all

Help text usually arrives as a tooltip or a bubble: easy to ignore when it is everywhere, and easy to resent when it interrupts. The Heron is embodied so that its presence carries information: when it has landed, a passage has been judged worth a second look. A heron fits the journal’s subject and its margins: a patient wading bird, narrow and upright, that watches before it acts.

How it behaves

Each Friday issue programs a few appearances, usually between none and four, each tied to a specific passage: a figure, a finding, an identification caveat. When that passage reaches the middle of your screen the Heron waits a moment, then flies in for about two seconds along a short arc from the page edge, keeping to the margin, and lands beside the passage below its research note. It then stays still. Its perch is labelled with the kind of intervention and a one-line title, so what it has to say is visible without any motion.

Choose the Heron (click, tap, or Tab to it and press Enter) and a compact note opens directly beneath it in the margin: a few sentences, sometimes a small diagram, and often a link to the relevant entry in the methods notebook. Esc or Close returns you to where you were. When you read well past that section the Heron leaves, and it may reappear later at the next programmed passage. It never leaves while its note is open.

What it can do

  • Ask the question a good referee would ask: What is the counterfactual here?
  • Define a term precisely: incidence, additionality, a shadow price.
  • Restate a paragraph as economic intuition.
  • Separate correlation from identification, or flag an exclusion restriction.
  • Sketch a tiny diagram, such as a tax wedge or a counterfactual path.
  • Point to the methods notebook entry that explains the tool being used.

What it deliberately does not do

  • It is not a chatbot. It does not converse, generate answers on demand or pretend to be a person; every note is written and checked with the issue.
  • It does not follow the cursor, animate while idle, or appear beside every article.
  • It never covers body text, blocks navigation, or opens anything you did not ask for.
  • It does not repeat itself: once you have opened a note, later visits show it perched quietly without a flight.

Why silence is part of the design

A companion that speaks about everything teaches you to ignore it. Most passages in a good issue do not need commentary, so the Heron’s default is to say nothing, and some weeks it may not appear at all. Its scarcity is what makes an appearance worth your attention.

How appearances are chosen

The Heron does not guess. Each week the editor marks the few places where readers most often go wrong or where a concept carries the argument: a causal claim resting on fixed effects, a burden whose incidence is not what it seems, a scenario mistaken for a forecast. The intervention is written for that exact passage, which is why it can be short.

Why it helps learning

The notes are timed to the moment of difficulty, when a question is most useful, and they usually ask before they tell, so you have a chance to reason first. Links into the methods notebook connect a single example to a reusable idea. Over many issues the same few questions (the counterfactual, the identifying variation, who ultimately pays) become habits.

How each issue programs it

The appearances live as structured data inside each issue (#companion-data, keyed by the issue’s date). Each entry names the target article and the passage that should trigger it, the kind of intervention (question, definition, intuition, method, caution, figure), a title, the note text, an optional diagram and an optional link. An issue with no entries is a silent issue.

You stay in control

  • Off: the Heron switch in the top bar turns it off everywhere in Loblolly & Logit until you turn it back on.
  • Dismiss: “Don’t show this again” in any note retires that one appearance permanently.
  • Seen: notes you have opened are remembered for that issue, so it will not fly in to show them again.

These choices are stored in this browser only.

Accessibility and reduced motion

The perched Heron is an ordinary button with a full spoken label (for example, “The heron asks: What is the counterfactual here? Open note”), reachable by keyboard in reading order beside its article. Notes are regions that receive focus when opened and return focus on close. If your system asks for reduced motion, or the screen is too narrow for a margin, the Heron never flies: it simply appears at its perch beside the passage. Nothing it offers depends on seeing it move.

Heron and Field Register

They are related but do different jobs. Field Notes and the Field Register introduce who does environmental economics, the people and institutions behind the week’s evidence, and accumulate into a directory. The Heron works on how to read the evidence, one passage at a time, and keeps nothing but your preferences.

HThe Commonplace

The publication stays permanent; your relationship to it is allowed to change.

The Commonplace is the private keeping layer shared by all three titles. A whole issue, Field Brief story, Loblolly & Logit article, figure, research note, Field Register entry, Heron note, Workbook question or worked solution can be kept without changing its source.

Keeping while reading. On desktop, a small keep action appears only when a keepable object is hovered or focused. On touch, press and hold the object. Keyboard users can reach the same action in reading order. The first action keeps the item; returning to it opens the quiet editor for your own thread names, an importance value from 0–5, and a short annotation.

Threads cross publications. Threads are names you invent, not categories imposed by Periodicals. A Field Brief story, a Loblolly & Logit article and a Workbook solution can therefore live together in one thread. The Commonplace uses those shared threads to show related material and, rarely, to bring an older item back into view.

Stable addresses. Every keepable object has a durable source address. Existing story, article and question IDs remain permanent; figures, research notes, Register entries and Heron interventions receive their own stable anchors. Future issue maintenance must never recycle or silently change a published keepable ID.

Private and local. The Commonplace lives in periodicals:commonplace:v1 in this browser. It does not sync to another device and nothing is sent to an account or outside service. To move it, use the Commonplace’s JSON export on one device and import it on another.

Backups and exports. The Commonplace exports a clean readable HTML document, plain Markdown, and structured JSON. JSON is the re-importable backup; imports merge with what is already present and do not silently delete existing kept items. The Commonplace quietly states when the last export is old, but does not interrupt reading to ask for one.

Returning. Every entry retains its exact source link, source documents and related methods/Register/Heron links when available. At most occasionally, the Commonplace may surface one highly rated older item or an older item from a thread you have just returned to. Silence is the default.
