# Illustration policy

A suggestion is one Tuesday or one Thursday recommendation. Each has one Full Edition, one Archive entry, one completion path and one **previously unpublished original finished illustration**. A small private reserve of approved original illustrations is permitted; it is not a collection of previously published stock. Subjects primarily feature Rupert, occasionally Bosch or Tilli, and do not need to depict the destination. Closer, bigger-day and alternative contingencies are subordinate fallbacks, not additional suggestions; they do not receive artwork or Archive entries. Repeat visits still receive new art. An edition's original, This Week display and Archive thumbnail may display the same asset because those are presentations of the same suggestion. Once actually released, the artwork bytes, registry and association are permanently frozen in `data/artwork-locks.json`.

Generate each illustration as part of the due Tuesday/Thursday publishing run, close to its guarded release. Do not deliberately send previews, thumbnails, descriptions or artwork announcements. Native image generation may inherently display its result in the task conversation; this cannot be called unseen/private, but it is not a reason to require an external API or a private artwork stockpile. Inspect the native result without attaching additional previews, then publish it with its recommendation only after all guards pass. Unpublished files, originals, metadata and drafts must still stay off public branches and URLs. See [_tools/PRIVATE-PREPARATION.md](_tools/PRIVATE-PREPARATION.md) for the existing recommendation handoff and isolated release procedure. Use 4:5 portrait artwork while preserving the approved composition, crop behavior, palette and print treatment. No design or artistic-direction change is authorized.

### Composition for future Tuesday and Thursday editions

Favor minimalist compositions most of the time. Choose deliberately rather than randomly:

- Minimal portraits against a solid burgundy/oxblood, ochre, olive, cream or navy field, with little or no scenery.
- Simple action: walking, running, swimming, carrying a tennis ball, investigating or resting, without elaborate staging.
- Cropped face, paws, profile, silhouette or other distinctive details. Preserve Rupert's likeness and keep essential features legible in This Week and Archive crops.
- Restrained environments with one or two meaningful landscape elements, generous negative space and a clear subject.
- Occasional elaborate woodland, mountain, river or destination-inspired prints only when the subject benefits from them. Use these sparingly so they feel special.

Before writing each new brief, visually review the latest six published illustrations (including withdrawn history), especially the immediately preceding Tuesday/Thursday pair. Compare pose, framing, subject scale, negative space, background treatment and dominant color. Record the intended differences in `artwork.brief`; do not rely on a generic instruction to “make it different.” Avoid repeatedly combining Rupert, mountains, water, trees and a circular sun. Vary composition within the established print family, not the illustration style. These instructions apply only to future artwork; existing published illustrations remain unchanged.

Exactly one Rupert by default: lean chocolate American Labrador, long muzzle, drop ears, mustard-orange collar. Companions appear only when deliberately chosen, each once: Bosch has a broader English Labrador head and light-blue collar; Tilli is a smaller Tennessee Treeing Brindle with a red collar. Banner dogs remain extremely simple outline vectors, a distinct functional use authorized in the refinement brief.

Use real supplied photos to confirm identity. The approved October 13, 2026 illustration is a successful reference for Rupert's face, expression, anatomy, body proportions, coat rendering and overall artistic execution. Preserve that likeness and execution. The sole anatomical refinement is his slimmer, sleeker, gradually tapered tail, consistent with his athletic American Labrador build; do not give him the heavier English Labrador otter tail. Preserve Bosch's distinct English Labrador proportions. This does not authorize new coat, anatomy or stylistic requirements. The published October 13 pixels remain unchanged.

Do not publish originals, camera filenames, GPS or private reference paths. Published illustration captions are **TRA Plate No. 001**, **TRA Plate No. 002**, and so on: actual three-digit integers, never literal format placeholders or destination descriptions. The existing photo registry stores `plate_no`, assigned chronologically on first publication and continued append-only. Withdrawn history retains its number; retry, destination change or an explicitly documented correction never renumbers the same illustration. Completed outings in Field Log use real photographs.

## Publishing a new suggestion

1. Write two concise `panels` (`label`, `text`) based on the suggestion's verified conditions and characteristics. Use factual, practical labels and copy. A season is suitability, not proof of a forecast. Capture the chosen wording in the edition so it stays faithful in the Archive.
2. Prepare artwork independently of the outing: a fresh opaque `image_id`, specific private composition `brief` and `characters` (normally `["rupert"]`). `node rupert/_tools/illustration.mjs --reserve PRIVATE_BRIEF.json PRIVATE_PROMPT.txt` lists only recent already-published references and saves a destination-independent 4:5 brief inside ignored storage. It does not call an API, emit an unused-art description or create art at page load. Use the native ChatGPT image-generation tool in the due publishing execution and obtain its real output file; do not use a separately billed API, substitute procedural drawings or redesign the established style.
3. Inspect the finished artwork for likeness, collar, dog count, restrained background, composition and distinction from recent editions. Earlier rejected proposals remain excluded; the specific approved exception above is assigned only to its own recommendation.
4. Import through the existing pixels-only photo pipeline **in private assembly** as `--kind plate --provenance editorial`, with meaningful alt text and an `Illustration` caption. Reserve ownership is `reserve/ID`, with actual `created_at`, `style_version:"atlas-engraved-3"`, visual-review evidence and a structural composition key. Keep stripped responsive JPEGs in the publishing run’s local ignored packet; a separate cloud artwork handoff/reserve is not required. At staging, the selected unused record receives `EDITION_ID/flagship` ownership; a failed candidate does not consume it. Actual publication freezes the pairing permanently.
5. Build/check/test/privacy audit. New release checks require a separate 4:5 plate and two frozen panels, existing owner checks, byte/normalized-pixel/perceptual duplicate checks and recent structural variety. Immutable pins protect every accepted historical association and image variant. These checks do not prove artistic originality or identity: genuine generation and substantive visual review remain required. No unused artwork may be committed or appear in public assets.

The four existing W40/W41 records are legacy prepared editions. The current W40 Tuesday illustration is replaced by the specific approved exception above; the other legacy records retain their existing artwork. New records cannot use that legacy exemption. Do not add IDs to the exemption to bypass the illustration requirement.

## Travel postcards

Travel automatically makes a private canvas postcard when a journey is mapped or saved. A bundled engraved Rupert element in the approved print family is combined locally with origin, destination, mode, terrain cues and route marks. No private itinerary is sent to an image-generation service. The caption and composition are included in backup/restore. Optional “Replace Artwork” accepts a JPEG/PNG/WebP that is resized and re-encoded; “Use Automatic Artwork” restores local composition. The reusable Travel element is not reused as a finished recommendation illustration.

## Full Edition fetch

Only Full Edition pages load the fetch script. A tiny fixed dock holds a muted tennis-green ball; click/tap or keyboard activation starts one throw → six-pose chase → brief exit → return carrying the ball → drop at the dock. No automatic triggers and no rare alternate behaviors enabled. Repeated presses during a loop are ignored. Escape, page hiding, resize or a changed motion preference resets the dock. Reduced motion uses a static response and live status text.
