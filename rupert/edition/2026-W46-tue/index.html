The Long Way Around Jones Lake

A four-mile circuit through Carolina bay forest, with pond cypress, boardwalks and tea-coloured water rather than a summit to reach.

Why This Week

After last week’s short western lake loop, this is a longer walk in a different landscape: pond cypress, dense bay vegetation and tea-coloured water in Bladen County. Start behind the picnic shelter and follow the white diamonds around Jones Lake. Four miles of roots, sand, boggy ground and boardwalks call for a slower pace than a paved lakeside path. Leave Salters Lake for another day and let Rupert’s energy and the footing decide whether to finish the circuit. Start early enough to be back before dark; do not depend on weekend visitor-centre access. The dated NWS outlook is sunny in the upper 50s both days, with nights near freezing. Give shaded boardwalks time to warm and turn back if they are slick.

Conditions

Saturday: Sunny, with a high near 57. Sunday: Sunny, with a high near 58. Saturday night: Clear, with a low around 31. Sunday night: Mostly clear, with a low around 31.

If it turns

Thunder, flooding, unsafe footing, strong wind or official closure: Postpone. No exposed trail or picnic is a storm alternative. Check official status and NWS before leaving.

Four Miles, Not Six

Follow the white-diamond Bay Trail from behind the picnic shelter. The out-and-back to Salters Lake adds two miles and is not included.

Expect Mixed Footing

Natural roots, sand and boggy soil alternate with boardwalks. Skip unsafe or flooded sections rather than detouring off-trail; carry water and finish well before dark.

Parking
Park in the signed day-use lot at 4117 N.C. 242 N. and start behind the picnic shelter. November hours are 8 a.m.–6 p.m.; finish in daylight. The visitor centre lists weekday hours; no weekend entry is assumed.

Dog Access

Leash
Pets on attended leash no longer than six feet; not on swim beach or inside buildings. Yield to other trail users.
Water
Carry all drinking water and a bowl. No swimming or wading is part of this outing.
Shade
Bay forest shade, with exposed picnic and lakeside areas.
Watch for
Boggy ground, wet roots and slick boardwalks; Mosquitos, ticks and snakes; remain on the trail; Lake edges and short November daylight
