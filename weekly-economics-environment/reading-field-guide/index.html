The Reading Field Guide · An editorial index

How a reading
takes shape.

Every reading begins with a question and evidence. The journal's recurring sections make its reasoning legible; occasional features bring the methods, margins, and mechanisms into sharper view.

This guide catalogs the forms a reading may take. It is not a checklist imposed on every article, nor a substitute for the edition's Methods & Concepts, which explains research techniques rather than editorial architecture.

Editorial sections

The analytical vocabulary of the journal: significance, interpretation, evidence boundaries, and transferable research ideas. These provide structure when relevant; none is included merely to fill space.

Special features

Occasional, purpose-built research instruments. Each must earn its place through the topic, the available evidence, or the particular decision under examination. They are not a rotating set of decorations.

Selection doctrine

The form follows the evidence.

Editorial sections articulate the argument. Special features examine a particular distributional question, decision margin, longer-run comparison, methodological assumption, or mechanism. A reading may use several, one, or none of the special features.

The feature registry offers a structured recommendation based on editorial tags and explicit eligibility. It does not fabricate an analysis, populate a graphic, infer an estimate, or publish a section automatically. The authoring and publishing process must supply adequately sourced material for every selected feature. Final inclusion remains subject to editorial verification.

New features belong in this cumulative guide after their function, evidence standard, and a genuine published specimen are defined. Older editions are preserved, not retroactively remodeled.
