01 / The Argument

Editorial Sections

Recurring analytical prose establishes why a finding matters, what economic reasoning adds, what the evidence can actually support, and which research concepts carry beyond the article.

02 / The Instruments

Special Features

Selective apparatuses for particular analytical problems. A feature is included only when it adds an identifiable intellectual function—not to create variety or fill a predetermined number of slots.

03 / The Distinctions

Related, Not Interchangeable.

Several features can address the same paper from different angles. The distinction is the question each one answers.

Economic Interpretation / The Mechanism MapInterpretation develops incentives and equilibrium logic in prose. The Map displays a sequence of mechanisms and labels the uncertain links, rather than claiming that arrows establish causality.
The Decision Margin / The Incidence LedgerThe Margin identifies feasible alternatives, relevant thresholds and opportunity costs. The Ledger presents affected parties as side-by-side comparison columns on desktop, with each party’s consequence and evidentiary status under its label. On narrow screens, those columns stack to remain readable.
Evidence Limits / The Assumption TestEvidence Limits evaluates what a study’s data, identification and external validity support overall. The Test isolates one consequential assumption and examines how a plausible violation would affect the result.
In the Research Notebook / The Estimation DeskThe Notebook teaches a transferable method or research question. The Desk presents a particular equation, outcome, regressors, estimand and identification caveats—not a generic statistical lesson.
The Heron / The Assumption TestThe Heron offers a short, reader-invoked methodological aside. The Test is a dedicated examination of a substantive assumption, not an extra note or an unperformed robustness claim.
The Long View / The Decision MarginThe Long View compares historic baselines, longer horizons and distinctly labeled scenarios. The Margin addresses the immediate policy or behavioral choice under available constraints.
Economic Figures / The Incidence LedgerEconomic Figures depict theoretical relationships, observed descriptive data, simulations or estimates, with clear evidence status. The Ledger is distributional accounting; a figure can accompany it without being part of it.
04 / Reading Architecture

Anatomy of a Reading

Each reading has an editorial backbone: a sourced finding, an explanation of its stakes, economic interpretation, and an explicit account of what the evidence can support. The order follows the argument—not a fixed checklist.

Special Features are added only if their particular contribution justifies the attention they require. A feature can be a Ledger, a model specification, a decision comparison, or an Economic Figure; no category is automatic.

05 / Editorial Rule

The Form Follows the Evidence.

Choose → Author → Verify → Publish

The registry evaluates evidence-backed, authored candidates to recommend relevant sections and special features. It does not generate substantive findings or publish a feature by itself. Each selected feature requires authored material, appropriate citations, clear evidence status, and editorial approval.

Approved editorial rule: begin with no special features, then include only those that add substantial, nonduplicative explanatory value and have sufficient evidence. Beyond two, require a written rationale for the additional feature and consider relocating optional detail. The Heron remains occasional; The Estimation Desk requires a meaningful model equation.

Spatial economy: each feature should occupy only the space its actual content requires. Three-stage chronologies should fill three balanced columns, never leave a blank fourth track. Avoid fixed-height blank panels, oversized inter-feature gaps, and unnecessarily wide empty columns; use compact vertical stacking or narrower grids where appropriate, while protecting typography, evidence labels, and responsive readability.

Published specimens are linked when the current edition contains a genuine example. Newly introduced formats remain labeled as prospective until they actually appear in a reading. Historical re-issues preserve earlier scholarship and publication dates.
