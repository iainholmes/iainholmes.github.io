The publication’s editorial reference manual

The Reading
Field Guide

A consistent analytical foundation. Distinctive special treatments. A form chosen for the evidence, rather than a template in search of content.

The Reading Index locates editions, subjects and actual features. This guide explains their purpose and boundaries.

Editorial Sections

The four core sections share one typographic system. They carry the argument, interpretation, inferential limits and research agenda; they are not counted as Special Features.

Why It Matters

Explains why the result matters for decisions, institutions, or welfare. Its boundary is practical significance: it does not attempt to establish an identification strategy or trace every market adjustment.

Economic Interpretation

Explains incentives, constraints, equilibrium responses and economic mechanisms. Its boundary is prose-based economic reasoning; an explicit multi-step diagram belongs in The Mechanism Map.

What the Evidence Does—and Does Not—Say

States design, measurement, uncertainty and identification limits. Its boundary is the overall inferential reach of data and design, rather than the intensive test of a single assumption.

In the Research Notebook

Connects a result to a reusable method, estimand or research question. Its boundary is transferable research literacy; a formal study-specific empirical model belongs at The Estimation Desk.

The selection standard

Aim for at least two substantive, nonredundant Special Features. This is an editorial target, never a reason to fabricate evidence. Document a justified exception. The Heron remains selective and does not satisfy the minimum on its own.

Assess analytical relevance, evidentiary support, distinctiveness, reader value, presentation suitability and information burden. Audit all eight families for broadly balanced use across the archive, then use variety as a tie-breaker among equally strong choices. Preserve original figures and document evidence-based exceptions rather than fill a quota. A third feature must justify its additional attention cost.

Choose → Author → Source → Compose → Validate → Inspect → Publish. Every future reading needs an authored decision record. Keyword matching and mechanical rotation cannot publish a feature.

Special Features

Each family has its own display type, information geometry, evidence labels and closing treatment. Body typography stays coherent with the journal. Examples below come from the six-edition archive.

The Incidence Ledger

Traces who pays, who gains, who adjusts, and how burdens are distributed. Its boundary is distribution across affected parties and the strength of supporting evidence, not a plotted theoretical curve or the policymaker's choice set.

When it earns its place. Use when heterogeneous effects or incidence can be supported by specific evidence or explicitly qualified economic reasoning.

The Long View

Places a finding against a defensible longer-run baseline, trend, or time horizon. Its boundary is temporal comparability, historical context and appropriately separated scenario versus observation.

When it earns its place. Use with comparable longitudinal evidence or a clearly identified scenario, not merely to add historical atmosphere.

Actual published examplesThe Grid Gets a $5.25 Billion Investment Test →A Climate Tipping Point Can Reverse the Geography of Damages →Browse every example in the Index ↗

The Decision Margin

Isolates the behavioral or policy choice at which incentives change. Its boundary is the immediate choice set, opportunity cost and behavioral threshold, not the ultimate distribution of burdens.

When it earns its place. Use when the relevant alternative, constraint, or threshold can be stated clearly.

The Heron

An occasional methodological companion that probes an assumption or interpretation. Its boundary is an optional concise methodological aside; it should not replace a substantive sensitivity analysis.

When it earns its place. Use sparingly, only when a pointed methodological question adds value and a sourced, concise answer is available.

The Assumption Test

Shows which assumptions a finding or model depends upon and what would change if they failed. Its boundary is one consequential premise, its plausible violation and how that alters interpretation—not a generic list of limitations.

When it earns its place. Use for important, testable or sensitivity-relevant assumptions; do not imply that a test has been run unless it has.

The Estimation Desk

Explains a consequential econometric specification or economic model, defines its terms and estimand or objective, and separates interpretation from identification. Its boundary is formal analytical structure: reported equations, reconstructed notation, illustrative theory and proposed designs must be distinguished.

When it earns its place. Use only when a model or empirical specification genuinely illuminates the study; label theoretical, proposed, estimated and illustrative equations accurately. Never invent estimates, standard errors or results.

The Mechanism Map

Traces the hypothesized path from an intervention or shock through behavioral and market adjustments to an outcome. Its boundary is the sequence and direction of channels, not a calibrated estimate of the size of each arrow.

When it earns its place. Use for multi-stage economic mechanisms; mark unverified links and avoid implying a diagram proves causality.

Economic Figures

Plots economic relationships, model comparisons, or empirical quantities with labeled axes, legends, and a clear evidence status. Its boundary is graphical illustration or measurement with clear provenance; it is separate from distributional accounting in The Incidence Ledger.

When it earns its place. Use when a visual substantially clarifies a quantitative or theoretical claim. Label schematic comparative statics, descriptive observations, simulations, and estimated effects distinctly. It may accompany but is not part of the Incidence Ledger.

Evidence has a visual grammar

Reported observations, estimated associations, model counterfactuals, illustrative theory and proposed designs are labeled explicitly. Ranges are not confidence intervals unless the source says so. A mechanism arrow is not proof of causality. A fiscal subtotal is not automatically social welfare.

Native mathematical notation defines its terms and interpretation, states its identifying assumptions and identifies its provenance. Unavailable full text is not represented as independently verified.

The composition follows the argument

The archive uses individually authored arrangements rather than a rotation of four layouts. A figure may organize the opening, a formal relationship may share a spread with its evidence, and a chronology may be full-width or deliberately vertical. Comparison spreads separate parties or accounting objects. Mechanism maps use connected nodes with qualified links; horizons distinguish observations, scenarios and conditional economic adjustment. Every reading has its own authored composition and reading-owned background. Features may share one field or be separated by standard material; their colors stay consistent within the reading. Economic Interpretation follows the finding. The notebook and Heron can occupy suitable available space. Sources come last, with horizontal references at wider widths, a copper-underlined descriptor and a short double rule. Interior vertical dividers are centered at 80% height and remain thinner and lighter than the continuous outer pillars. Full-width rules separate readings; no rule crosses a reading number. Short panels close with their content, and responsive tracks contain no empty fourth station.

Original edition dates, reading anchors, source links and defensible research claims remain part of the permanent record.
